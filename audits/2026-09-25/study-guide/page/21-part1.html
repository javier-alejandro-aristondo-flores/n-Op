Part 1 What the suite asks

The suite is a set of neural operators, each trained on density functional theory (DFT) calculations of crystals, and each judged against simple methods fixed before it trained. Its plan, test-suite.md, was built on 19 August 2026, before any model code existed. For every entry it names the task, the data, how the data is split, the simple methods to beat, and by how much.

What counts as a model here

An operator maps a whole function to a whole function: a field goes in, a field comes out, and the output can be asked for at any point or on any grid. An ordinary neural network maps a fixed list of numbers to a fixed list of numbers. The plan admits only operators. Fixed-vector predictors are excluded whatever their pedigree, and single numbers such as band gaps enter only as read-outs of a predicted function, never as training targets.

The flagship is the example to keep in mind: it takes the charge density on a three-dimensional grid and returns the electron localization field on a grid.

Floors and bars

A floor is a simple method every model must beat: copy the closest training example, fit a straight line, predict the average. Each floor is computed on exactly the runs the model is judged on, before the model trains. A bar is the margin required over a floor, written down in advance, such as "25% better than the ridge floor". Missing a kill bar removes the entry, or that task, and the miss is recorded as a dead end with its evidence.

Because floors and bars exist before training, no threshold can be moved after a result is seen. The audit checked the dates: every judged model's bars were committed before its first training run.

One seed

Each configuration was trained once, from one random seed. On the branch–trunk network's task, a twelve-run seed sweep measured the spread from run to run at 14–35% of the median. A difference of a few percent between two single runs is therefore noise, and the results page says so beside its numbers.

The rules for finishing, and the pause

On 16 September Javier and the PI set the rules: failures are acceptable and are marked as dead ends rather than ground on; one seeded run per configuration; every escalation the plan names is tried once before an entry is called dead; and the two entries without a package each get a minimal build. On 25 September the PI paused all training and model development until what exists is verified. The last training run stopped at 00:18 on 18 September.

Words used throughout

WordMeaning here
runOne VASP calculation: a folder of output files for one crystal geometry and one functional.
fieldA number at every point of a three-dimensional grid over the crystal's repeating cell.
charge densityHow much electron charge sits at each point. The primary output of a DFT calculation.
localization fieldThe electron localization function: a number from 0 to 1 at each point, high where electrons are pinned (bonds, lone pairs), low where they roam.
local potentialThe potential an electron feels at each point, in electronvolts.
magnetizationSpin-up minus spin-down density; zero except in magnetic runs.
density of statesA curve over energy counting the electron states at each energy. The gap in it is the band gap.
functionalThe approximation DFT uses. The cheap one (PBE) underestimates band gaps; the accurate hybrid (HSE) costs 10–100 times more.
pseudopotentialThe licensed file (POTCAR) describing each element's inner electrons. Never copied off /Pool.
unitA group of runs that must stay together in one split (Part 3). Every score is a median over units.
foldOne of five slices of the data. Fold 0 is the one models are judged on.
relative L2The size of the error divided by the size of the truth.
mean absolute errorThe average absolute difference between prediction and truth, abbreviated MAE.
ridgeLinear regression with a small penalty on the coefficients, solved in one step.
POD basisProper-orthogonal decomposition: the principal components of a set of fields. A rank-32 basis keeps the top 32.
checkpointSaved weights, optimizer state and curves, written during training so a run can resume.
