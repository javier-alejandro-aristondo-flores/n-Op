I.1Factorized Fourier operator, the flagship

pass

charge density → electron localization field

Fed
Each run's two spin densities, log-compressed, plus six channels describing the cell's shape (its lattice Gram matrix), on the 80³ grid. These are cut to 40³ by dropping high Fourier modes before the first layer, which is exact because that layer acts point by point.
Built
A point-by-point lift to 64 channels, then twelve factorized spectral layers keeping 19 Fourier modes per axis (the plan's "20³" is one more than a 40-point axis can hold), then a head that keeps every output between 0 and 1. 11.55M parameters.
Data
The cubic block. Trained on folds 2–4 (196 runs), stopped by fold 1 (65 runs, 24 units), judged on fold 0 (76 runs, 29 units). One run per step, single precision, one seed.
Trained
24,404 steps in 3.94 hours, 3.6 GB on the card. The last stage stopped early, at 3,100 of its 14,201 steps.
Floors
Mean absolute error, measured before training: semilocal ridge 0.0976 (the plan's kill anchor), per-shell filter 0.0830, training-mean template 0.0160, nearest-run copy 0.0062.
Bars
Kill at half the ridge, at most 0.0488. Pattern rule: 20% better than the ridge, at most 0.0781. Added before training: beat the template, beat the copy, and a stretch level at half the template, at most 0.0080.
Result
Mean absolute error 0.00217 (relative L2 1.05%, structural similarity 0.9998). Defect set 0.00233, strains 0.00070.
Verdict
All five levels pass: 97.8% better than the ridge, 65% better than the copy.
After
The grading deep dive reproduced every number to six digits from the checkpoint with independently written code. It also showed that fold 0 holds three different tests: plain strained diamond, defects of familiar elements, and nine dopant elements never seen in training. The audit re-scored 0.002170 exactly. The "8.0e-5" beside it should read 8.1e-5 (Part 5).
Same model, other tasks
Local potential: floors measured, never trained. The anchor floor, Hartree plus a semilocal ridge, scores 47.96%, so the bar is 23.98%. Parametric form (II.4): strain → density; bracketing interpolation scores 0.091% pooled, so the kill bar is 0.064%. Never trained; the plan predicts the floor wins.

I.3The weight-sharing ladder

four rungs judged

charge density → electron localization field, as I.1

The question
Does applying one layer twelve times (weight-tying) do as well as twelve separate layers? Does solving for the layer's fixed point, the state it maps to itself, add anything beyond that?
The rungs
RungParametersStepsTimeValidation lossMAE
explicit, twelve layers11.55M24,4043.94 h8.1e-50.00217
weight-tied, input not injected0.96M23,8042.69 h1.2e-40.00297
weight-tied, input injected0.96M28,504resumed across power losses1.0e-40.00271
one explicit layer, matched parameters0.96M35,5050.52 h3.9e-40.01004

Reading
Weight-tying wins at matched parameters: the tied rungs beat the single layer 3.4–3.7 times over. It loses at matched width: the twelve-layer stack is still better. Injecting the input at every application buys about 9%. The fixed point converges (100% of runs, in 11 iterations, on both folds) but is less accurate than every comparator. So: weight-tying yes, implicit depth no.
Verdict
The four unrolled rungs pass all five levels except the single layer, which fails the copy and the stretch levels. The fixed-point result exists only in the ladder's own working copy, uncommitted.
What broke
The shared fixed-point code had four defects that surfaced only on its first runs on the card:
  • it asked numpy to read a tensor that lived on the card;
  • its acceleration step ran on the processor;
  • it never fed the input back in, so every input reached the same fixed point and the model could only output a constant;
  • its stopping tolerance was absolute, which on a four-million-entry state meant parts per million.
Then the layer turned out not to contract at its default initialization, so three stability rungs were built. The first, spectral clipping (capping each mode's gain so the map contracts), passed its probe and was trained.
Audit
Re-scored 0.002966, 0.002706 and 0.010043 exactly. Found The fixed-point report's verdict line says "unconverged" while its own health check says 100% converged. Found The weight-tied rung returned a stage 2.0% worse on validation than one it had already kept.

I.4Galerkin transformer

killed

its first gate: six perovskite lattice factors → charge density at query points

Fed
The six lattice factors as constant channels plus periodic coordinate features on 32³ tokens, answering 64³ query points. The target is standardized voxel by voxel.
Built
Attention without a softmax (queries times the product of keys and values, normalized over tokens), then a cross-attention decoder at the query points. Width 128, four heads, four layers, 374,145 parameters.
Data
The perovskite angle sweep. Judged on its 25 fold-0 runs and on 27 interior levels of the angle arm.
Trained
A two-hour cap, budgeted at 52,283 steps from a measured 0.138 seconds per step, 1.5 GB. It stopped early after 33,170 steps plus the probe, about 78 minutes. Validation sat at the mean field the whole time.
Floors and bars
Nearest-angle copy 0.0853, bar at most 0.0426. Linear-in-angle interpolation 0.0264, bar at most 0.0132. The training-mean field, 0.1755, as context.
Result
0.1780 and 0.1191: no better than the mean field.
Verdict
Killed at its first gate by its own pre-registration. Its second stage, the localization task, never ran.
What broke
Three defects were found and fixed before the verdict:
  • the decoder's queries carried only coordinates, so training made the output blind to the lattice factors (outputs for two structures 27% apart at initialization, 4e-8 apart after training);
  • the electron-count correction sat inside the training loss;
  • the density's spikes at the atomic cores dominated the squared error (the top 5% of voxels carried 77% of it), fixed by standardizing voxel by voxel.
The first gate run is void.
Hypothesis
Recorded before the verdict, never tested: six numbers and four Fourier modes per axis cannot build structure concentrated at atomic cores. The branch–trunk network solves the same task because its basis hands it that structure.
Audit
Re-scored exactly. The interpolation comparison is in-sample on 24 of 27 levels, and the copy floor misses the symmetry copies; the kill stands either way.

II.1Branch–trunk network

pass on strain

strain or lattice factors → charge density

How it works
A branch network reads the run's parameters into a vector; a trunk turns a query point into features; their product is the field at that point.
Configurations
  • principal_component: a fixed basis of the top 32 components; its linear version is exactly the ridge floor.
  • proper_orthogonal: the same, fit on fields standardized voxel by voxel.
  • canonical: a learned trunk over coordinates, answerable on any grid.
  • energy_trunk: the density-of-states task, on its own sheet below.
Strain task
The 40³ block, per functional. Judged on 88 test runs over 22 orbits. The fixed-basis configurations trained on 608 runs per functional for 32,000 steps; the learned trunk trained on 928 runs across every grid shape.
Floors and bars
Ridge 0.00414 (cheap) and 0.00411 (accurate), to beat by 25%. Nearest-neighbor copy 0.00896, to beat by 2×. The basis alone limits error to 0.000017.
Result
Relative L2, cheap / accurate:
  • principal_component: 0.000821 / 0.001092;
  • proper_orthogonal: 0.000819 / 0.001122;
  • canonical: 0.001896 / 0.002413 on the 40³ block, and 0.002339 / 0.002766 on all 124 test runs across nine grid shapes.
Fourteen of fourteen comparisons pass. A seed sweep found the two fixed-basis configurations indistinguishable: a paired difference of 0.5% and 1.8%, against spreads of 14–35%.
Perovskite task
Fold 0: ridge 0.0423, copy 0.0853; the three configurations 0.0183, 0.0189 and 0.0235, all passing. The holdouts labeled extrapolation read 0.0937, 0.1264 and 0.0434 for factor 0.8, and 0.0584, 0.0606 and 0.0499 for factor 1.2.
What broke
The first perovskite proper_orthogonal rows projected standardized fields onto a basis fit on raw ones, which showed up as an 18-fold blowup in the high-frequency band on every split. It was caught before it shipped and pinned by a regression test.
Audit
Every strain number reproduced; the fixed-basis rows were rebuilt from the weights kept in the figure caches, since those runs saved no checkpoint. Found The perovskite rows are compromised: every fold-0 run has a same-crystal copy in training, and a copy that knows the cell's symmetry scores 1.7e-7, roughly 100,000 times better than the best configuration. The perovskite passes measure nothing the labels claim.

VI.1Energy trunk, a branch–trunk configuration

no bar

strain → density of states over energy

Fed
The six strain components plus the functional as a seventh input. The target is each run's curve rebuilt from its eigenvalues with 0.175 eV smearing, on a window from −28 to +8 eV around the top of the valence band: 601 points, 0.06 eV apart.
Data
1,856 training runs; judged on 248 test runs over 30 orbits, both functionals pooled.
Floors
Training mean 0.392 (whole window) and 0.757 (band edges); ridge 0.368 and 0.518.
Result
Curve L1 0.206 over the whole window, 0.265 at the band edges. A variant weighting the band edges five times scores 0.208 and 0.193.
Verdict
The results page says "pass, 44% better", but the plan sets no bar for this task.
Audit
Found A nearest-neighbor copy of a training curve scores 0.149 and 0.171, better than the model on both windows (computed 2 October). The gap-edge read-out is an artifact of the smearing: every true curve crosses its threshold at +0.020 eV.
Why whole-window looks weak
467 of the 601 bins are valence states that strain barely moves, so they dilute the score. The signal lives at the band edges.

II.2Two-input network

killed

charge density and local potential → electron localization field

Fed
The top 32 components of the log-compressed density and of the local potential with its mean removed.
Built
Two branches whose outputs multiply entry by entry, a trunk queried at points, and a head bounded between 0 and 1: 245,632 parameters. Its twin is the same network with the potential branch held constant: 224,896.
Data
The cubic block; judged on fold 0, 29 units.
Trained
Budgets of 33,650 steps each, matched on purpose; both stopped early at 22,090. About half an hour each, at about 0.11 seconds per step, 0.8 GB.
Floors and bars
Semilocal ridge 0.097618, to beat by 20% (the pattern rule). Template 0.015957; copy 0.006164. Decisive: beat its own twin, 0.014553, by 5%.
Result
0.016454: 13.1% worse than its twin.
Verdict
Killed on its decisive bar. It passes the pattern rule, 83.1% better than the ridge, but does not beat the training-mean template. The potential added nothing at a matched budget.
What broke
  • Its first run never trained. The potential's coefficients entered in raw units, about 48 times the density's scale, so the bounded head sat at exactly zero with no gradient. Fixed by standardizing them.
  • Twin and member were first given different budgets: a warm-cache cost probe measured 0.107 seconds per step against 0.451. They were then matched at 33,650.
Audit
Re-scored exactly. Its fixed bases are fit on folds 1–4, so the validation fold sits inside its own components; the verdict against the twin is unaffected. Pending Its floors figure draws the pass lines at a zero margin, and the cross-member table shows no verdict for it.

II.3Manifold decoder

killed

strain → charge density, decoded point by point

Fed
The six strain components plus the functional, sampled at points on every grid shape.
Built
An encoder into a 128-number vector, then a decoder in which the value at a point is a nonlinear function of that vector and the point's coordinates, proven not reducible to a linear map of the vector. 272,001 parameters.
Trained
About 10,200 steps in seven minutes on the card.
Judged on
423 interior levels of the strain sweeps (leave one level out), plus the committed test split (22 units) and every grid shape (30 units).
Floors and bars
Bracketing interpolation 0.000911, bar at most 0.000607. Radial basis 0.000991, bar at most 0.000661. Ridge 0.004225 and copy 0.019876 as context.
Result
0.002452, 2.7 times worse than interpolation. Grid transfer 1.32 times against a bar of 1.3: a narrow miss.
Verdict
Killed, as the plan predicted: on an interpolation split, a straight line between neighboring levels is nearly exact.
Audit
Re-scored exactly. 337 of the 423 levels are its own training data, and 66 levels average three differently oriented shear fields. The kill stands on the 86 levels holding no training run (−280.2%) and without the shear arms (−125.4%).

IV.1Correction network

pass, hollow

cheap-functional density → accurate-functional density

Fed
The cheap density's top 32 components.
Built
A small network that starts as the identity and predicts the correction's top 32 components, held at zero mean. 24,865 parameters.
Data
The strain atlas's 40³ block: 608 training, 83 validation and 88 test runs (22 orbits).
Trained
20,000 steps on the shared schedule.
Floors
Identity 0.011131, so the kill bar is half of it, at most 0.005565. Global affine 0.010466. Ridge from the strain 0.000049. Ridge from the cheap density's components 0.000005. The basis's own limit 0.000002.
Result
0.002079: 81.3% better than identity.
Verdict
Passes the plan's bar. But plain linear regression on the same inputs is 406 times better: on this holdout every test point is a small step from a training point, the correction is almost exactly linear, and the network adds nothing.
Audit
Re-scored exactly. Found Its floors figure keeps only one of five floors, because every comparison was filed under the same name. The picture shows the network beating the only floor drawn.

IV.3Conformal wrapper

valid

a prediction → the same prediction with an interval guaranteed to cover

How
Calibrated at level 0.90 with the orbit as unit, on 23 validation orbits, then applied to the correction network's 22 test orbits.
Result
Coverage 0.977 against a guaranteed 0.900–0.942: valid, and conservative.
Audit
Found results.json records the coverage as 1.0, which is a median of per-orbit hit-or-miss values and reads 1.0 for any coverage above one half. It also stores the guarantee band where the confidence interval belongs. The 0.977 appears nowhere in the file.

never trained

Built

Data
The defect campaign; judged on fold 0, 42 runs in 21 units. 18 of those runs carry an element absent from every training fold.
Floors
Superposed atoms 0.154149 (0.161438 on the unseen elements). A reduced density-fitting ridge, 0.111713, whose spread runs about a hundred times its median: a badly conditioned fit. Nearest-structure copy 0.067496.

Status
Never trained. The silicon runs labeled "H" (Part 2) matter here, because this model keys atoms by element.
